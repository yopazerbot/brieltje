Our legitimate interests are a working, safe game without abuse, knowing whether it's used, and organising a meetup and the game night for those who want one. We keep what we use for them as small as we can. You can object to them at any time (section 9). How we weighed each of them against your interests is in our legitimate interest assessments.

A player with an account can open a Zombies room: a game of its own, apart from the open world. Everyone playing together sees the list of rooms, with the name of whoever opened each one, its place and how many play in it. Rooms live in the server's memory only and close once they are empty.

On a console you can log in with your phone: the console shows a short code and a QR code, and your phone, logged in to your account, says yes. The code and the console's waiting request live in the server's memory only, for 5 minutes at most. The login log notes that a screen was logged in this way, with the IP addresses involved.

If a live fighter loses their connection during Zombies, the server keeps a link to their existing sign-in for up to 15 seconds so they can return to the same game. This reconnect information stays in memory only and is deleted when the grace period expires or the game ends. It cannot bring a downed player back to life.

3. Counting visits

When you open the game or the interest page, the server counts the visit. To count each visitor once per day, it makes a code from your IP address and your browser's name. That code is mixed with a random value that changes every day. When you start a game, the server also counts you once that day as a player, with a code made the same way. The day's random value and the day's codes are kept in the server's database for that day only, so a restart of the server doesn't count you twice. The codes can't be turned back into an address without a key that the database doesn't hold. Shortly after midnight, the server deletes that day's value and codes. After that, nobody can match yesterday's visitors to today's, or trace them back to an address.

The game also tells the server when a game starts, each minute while you play, and when you leave. That message holds what you chose in the game: the mode, the controls, alone or together, and the language. When you start alone, it also holds the name you typed. The server writes that name with your IP address in the login log, which it keeps 90 days. The message also carries a random number that the page makes fresh at every visit, so the admin can see how many people are playing alone right now and what they play. The server keeps that number in memory only, and forgets it a minute after you stop. Nothing else about your device or about you is sent.

What we keep are totals per day and per hour of the day: visits, visitors, players, games started, minutes played and how many chose each option. Those totals say nothing about any person. No cookies or browser storage are used for this.

If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, you are not counted at all. Your name is then not logged when you start alone either.

4. The interest page (brieltje.be/interested) and the game night

If you sign up there for a meetup to play together, we keep what you fill in:

  • your name and email address (required);
  • your street, if you give it;
  • when it would suit you, how many of you would come, and your comments.

We use it only to organise the meetup and to contact you about it. Only the admin sees it, on a page behind a login. Unless you untick the box, we email you once the meetup is planned, with the date and the place. We send nothing else, never in a way that shows your address to others, and you can turn it off any time through your own link.

If you also want your house in the game, we keep the plans you upload (PDFs or images) and your consent, with the time you gave it. You confirm four things: you live there or have permission, the outside of the 3D model may be made public, you know the plans themselves are never published, and you agree that AI helps build the model. We use the plans only to build the model. You also choose what happens to the inside (see "Your home in the game" below). That choice is private unless you pick public and confirm it separately.

Yoshi builds the models with the help of Claude, an AI service from Anthropic. Your plans are sent to Anthropic for that. Yoshi has switched off the use of this data to train Anthropic's AI. Anthropic keeps it only for a limited time and protects transfers outside the EU with the EU's adequacy decisions or standard contractual clauses, as its privacy policy describes.

The plans are kept in a private storage bucket at Railway in Amsterdam. Only the admin and the AI service above get to see them, and they are never published. The outside of the model we make is public: it is in the game and in the project's public source code on GitHub.

The page gives you a private link to look at or withdraw your sign-up. Withdrawing removes your details and your plans at once. You can also email us. If you withdraw your consent for your house, we take its model out of the game and out of the current source code. Copies others already made of the public source code can't be recalled. Withdrawing doesn't make what we did before it unlawful.

Your home in the game: the outside and the inside

The outside of your house is public. Anyone who plays can see it, and it is in the project's public source code.

The inside is private by default. It is locked with an access code that only you get. Whoever types the right code at your door can walk in, for 8 hours, tied to their session. You can give the code to family and friends, and the admin can make a new one at any time, which ends the old one.

Someone who types the right code while logged in to an account keeps access with that account, on every device, without typing the code again. A new code ends that access too, and so does revoking the code or changing its settings. The admin can also remove one account's access, or everyone's.

The inside is not in the public source code or in the public game files. It is stored encrypted, and only the game server holds the key. The server sends the inside to a player only after the right code. The code is not kept: only a hash of it. We also count how often a code was right or wrong, and when. No name or IP address is kept with those counters.

The inside is public only if you explicitly choose that, on the interest page or later through your own link, and confirm it separately. Public means anyone can see it, in the game and in the public source code. You can switch between private and public at any time through your own link. When you go back to private, we lock the inside behind a code again and take it out of the current public source code. Copies others already made of the public source code can't be recalled.

The legal basis for the inside is your consent (art. 6(1)(a)). You can withdraw it at any time, through your own link or by email. That doesn't make what we did before unlawful.

The street suggestions are looked up through our server in the Flemish address register (Digitaal Vlaanderen). Only what you type in the street field is sent there. Your IP address isn't.

5. Where the data comes from, and whether you have to give it

Everything we use comes from you or from your browser, except the street suggestions, which come from the Flemish address register.

Plans of your home can show things about the people you live with. Only upload them if they agree.

No law or contract obliges you to give us anything. Playing alone needs no name. Playing together needs a name, which may be a nickname. An account also needs an email address and a password. You don't need an account to play together. Signing up for a meetup needs a name and an email address, so we can reach you. The game night also asks your first name, your street and how many of you come. Without them you can't sign up. Everything else on the form is optional.

We make no automated decisions about you, and we don't profile you. The only automatic measures are a short pause after too many wrong passwords, joins or new accounts from one place, to stop guessing and floods, and the block on visits from outside Belgium (section 7).

7. Who else receives data

  • Railway (Railway Corporation, United States) hosts the game, its database and the plan storage, on servers in Amsterdam. It works on our behalf under a data processing agreement. That agreement covers any access from outside the EU with the EU's standard contractual clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Cloudflare delivers the site and protects it against attacks, on our behalf. It lets in only visitors whose IP address is in Belgium, and blocks the rest. This is a fixed rule about countries, not a decision about you. If you want to play from elsewhere, email us. It may process data outside the EU under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and the EU's standard contractual clauses.
  • GitHub hosts the public source code, which includes the outside of 3D models of houses whose residents agreed (and the inside, only where they chose to make it public). It receives no other data from us.
  • Digitaal Vlaanderen receives the text you type in the street field, to suggest streets, and nothing else.
  • Anthropic (Claude, an AI service) receives the plans of those who agreed to it, to help build the 3D model of their house. Yoshi has switched off training Anthropic's AI on this data. See Anthropic's privacy policy.
  • Our email provider handles emails between you and us.
  • Other players see your name, where you are, what you do and your chat while you play together. They see the rooms that are open and who opened them. Anyone who opens the game sees the board of best Zombies runs: the names of accounts with their best wave, the zombies they beat, how many played and the day. At a meetup, a scoreboard with the names and scores of those playing together may be shown on a big screen.

We don't sell your data or share it with anyone else, unless the law requires it, for example to report serious abuse to the police.

8. Security

Every connection is encrypted (HTTPS). Passwords and the secret in your sign-up link are stored only as hashes (passwords with scrypt), so even we can't read them back. A forgotten password can't be sent to you: the admin can give you a new one. Plans sit in a private bucket that only the admin's login opens, through links that expire after a few minutes. The private inside of a home is stored encrypted, and only the game server holds the key. Limits per address stop floods and guessing. If a breach ever puts your data at risk, we report it to the Data Protection Authority within 72 hours, and we tell you directly if the risk to you is high.

9. Your rights

You can ask us, for free:

  • to see the data we have about you and get a copy (access);
  • to correct it (rectification);
  • to delete it (erasure);
  • to use it less while something is being sorted out (restriction);
  • to get what you gave us in a common format, to take elsewhere (portability);
  • to stop using it where we rely on our legitimate interest (objection);
  • to withdraw your consent at any time. The sign-up link does this in one click.

With an account you can do two of these yourself, in the game's menu under Your account: see your name and email address, and delete the account with everything that belongs to it.

Email yoshi@brieltje.be. We answer within one month. For difficult requests we may need two more months, and if so we tell you why. We may ask you to show it's you, for example by writing from the email address you signed up with. For the game, tell us the name you played under and roughly when. We can't find you in the visit counts, because they hold nothing about any person.

You can also complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority: Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit, Drukpersstraat 35, 1000 Brussels, contact@apd-gba.be, dataprotectionauthority.be. You don't have to ask us first, though we'd like the chance to fix it.

10. Children

Playing alone needs no age check. When it starts, the server notes the name typed and the IP address in its log, also for children. A parent or guardian can ask us by email to delete it. Playing together, making an account, the interest page and the game night are for people aged 13 and over, which you confirm with a tick box. For anyone younger, a parent or guardian decides and signs up for them. Sending plans of a house is for adults (18 and over). At a meetup or the game night, anyone under 16 comes with a parent or guardian.

11. Changes

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. If a change affects what you gave us on the interest page or for the game night, we email you about it first.

3. Bezoeken tellen

Open je het spel of de interessepagina, dan telt de server het bezoek. Om elke bezoeker één keer per dag te tellen, maakt hij een code van je IP-adres en de naam van je browser. Die code wordt gemengd met een willekeurige waarde die elke dag verandert. Begin je een spel, dan telt de server je die dag ook één keer als speler, met een code die op dezelfde manier gemaakt is. De willekeurige waarde en de codes van de dag staan enkel die dag in de databank van de server, zodat een herstart van de server je niet twee keer telt. Zonder een sleutel die niet in de databank zit, kunnen de codes niet terug omgezet worden in een adres. Kort na middernacht wist de server de waarde en de codes van die dag. Daarna kan niemand de bezoekers van gisteren nog koppelen aan die van vandaag, of ze terugvoeren tot een adres.

Het spel laat de server ook weten wanneer een spel begint, elke minuut terwijl je speelt, en wanneer je weggaat. Dat bericht bevat wat je in het spel koos: de modus, de besturing, alleen of samen, en de taal. Begin je alleen, dan bevat het ook de naam die je typte. De server schrijft die naam met je IP-adres in het aanmeldlogboek, dat hij 90 dagen bewaart. Het bericht draagt ook een willekeurig getal dat de pagina bij elk bezoek opnieuw maakt, zodat de beheerder kan zien hoeveel mensen nu alleen spelen en wat ze spelen. De server houdt dat getal enkel in zijn geheugen, en vergeet het een minuut nadat je stopt. Er wordt verder niets over je toestel of over jou gestuurd.

Wat we bewaren, zijn de totalen per dag en per uur van de dag: bezoeken, bezoekers, spelers, gestarte spellen, gespeelde minuten en hoeveel mensen elke keuze maakten. Die totalen zeggen niets over wie dan ook. Hiervoor worden geen cookies of browseropslag gebruikt.

Stuurt je browser Global Privacy Control of Do Not Track, dan word je helemaal niet geteld. Je naam komt dan ook niet in het logboek als je alleen begint.

Je huis in het spel: de buitenkant en de binnenkant

De buitenkant van je huis is openbaar. Iedereen die speelt kan hem zien, en hij zit in de openbare broncode van het project.

De binnenkant is standaard privé. Hij is vergrendeld met een toegangscode die enkel jij krijgt. Wie de juiste code aan je deur typt, kan 8 uur lang binnen, gekoppeld aan zijn of haar sessie. Je kunt de code aan familie en vrienden geven, en de beheerder kan op elk moment een nieuwe maken, waardoor de oude vervalt.

Wie de juiste code typt terwijl die op een account ingelogd is, houdt met dat account toegang, op elk toestel, zonder de code opnieuw te typen. Een nieuwe code beëindigt ook die toegang, net als de code intrekken of de instellingen ervan wijzigen. De beheerder kan ook de toegang van één account weghalen, of die van iedereen.

De binnenkant staat niet in de openbare broncode of in de openbare spelbestanden. Hij staat versleuteld opgeslagen, en enkel de spelserver heeft de sleutel. De server stuurt de binnenkant pas naar een speler na de juiste code. De code zelf bewaren we niet: enkel een hash ervan. We tellen ook hoe vaak een code juist of fout was, en wanneer. Er wordt geen naam of IP-adres bij die tellers bewaard.

De binnenkant is enkel openbaar als je dat uitdrukkelijk kiest, op de interessepagina of later via je eigen link, en dat apart bevestigt. Openbaar betekent dat iedereen hem kan zien, in het spel en in de openbare broncode. Je kunt via je eigen link op elk moment wisselen tussen privé en openbaar. Ga je terug naar privé, dan vergrendelen we de binnenkant opnieuw met een code en halen we hem uit de huidige openbare broncode. Kopieën die anderen al van de openbare broncode maakten, kunnen we niet terughalen.

De rechtsgrond voor de binnenkant is je toestemming (art. 6, lid 1, a). Je kunt ze op elk moment intrekken, via je eigen link of per mail. Dat maakt niet onrechtmatig wat we ervoor deden.

8. Beveiliging

Elke verbinding is versleuteld (HTTPS). Wachtwoorden en het geheim in je inschrijvingslink bewaren we enkel als hash (wachtwoorden met scrypt), zodat zelfs wij ze niet kunnen teruglezen. Een vergeten wachtwoord kunnen we je niet sturen: de beheerder kan je een nieuw geven. Plannen staan in een afgeschermde opslag die enkel de login van de beheerder opent, via links die na een paar minuten vervallen. De privé-binnenkant van een woning staat versleuteld opgeslagen, en enkel de spelserver heeft de sleutel. Grenzen per adres stoppen een stortvloed aan verzoeken en het raden van codes. Brengt een datalek ooit je gegevens in gevaar, dan melden we dat binnen 72 uur aan de Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit. Is het risico voor jou hoog, dan laten we het jou ook rechtstreeks weten.
